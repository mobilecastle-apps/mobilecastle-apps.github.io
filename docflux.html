DocFlux — Privacy Policy

Privacy Policy for DocFlux

App: DocFlux: PDF Tools Package name: com.mobilecastle.docflux Publisher: Mobile Castle Effective date: 19 November 2026

This Privacy Policy explains what information the DocFlux app (“DocFlux”, “the app”, “we”, “us”) does and does not handle, and how. Please read it together with the policies of the third parties named below.

By using DocFlux you agree to the practices described here.

Summary (plain language)

  • DocFlux has no user accounts and no sign-up. We do not ask for your name, email, phone number or any similar personal detail.
  • Your documents stay on your phone. PDFs and pictures you open are processed on your phone, and DocFlux never uploads them, their text or their translations. Mobile Castle runs no server that receives them.
  • No camera, storage or contacts permission. The scanner runs inside Google Play services, and files are opened through Android’s own pickers.
  • Some features use Google services: ads from Google AdMob (with a consent form where the law requires one), and Google ML Kit for the scanner, text recognition and translation, which sends Google usage statistics, never your documents. Translation language packs are downloaded from Google only after you agree.
  • No purchases in this version. DocFlux offers nothing to buy: it does not connect to Google Play Billing and does not ask for the billing permission. Premium features are coming soon.

The rest of this policy gives the detail behind that summary.

Information DocFlux itself collects

DocFlux does not collect personal data on any server of ours, because Mobile Castle does not run a server that receives your data. Specifically:

  • No account or identity data. There is no login.
  • No upload of documents. The PDFs and pictures you open, the pages you scan, the text DocFlux reads from them, your translations, passwords you type and the files you make are not sent to us or to anyone else by DocFlux.
  • No analytics or crash reporting of our own. DocFlux does not embed an analytics, crash-reporting or tracking service of Mobile Castle. The Google services described below are independent third parties with their own data handling.

Your documents

  • Opening files. You choose a PDF or pictures with Android’s file picker or photo picker. DocFlux copies the file into its own private working folder on your phone and works on that copy. The original is never changed: every tool writes a new file.
  • Processing. Viewing, merging, splitting, compressing, converting, page editing, password protection (AES-256), unlocking with a password you know, redaction, comparison, text extraction, text recognition (OCR) and translation all run on your phone.
  • Working copies are deleted when a tool is done with them; anything left over is deleted the next time the app starts after a day.
  • Saving and sharing happen only when you ask, through Android’s own save picker or share sheet. If you save to an online storage app (for example Google Drive) or share with another app, that app’s own privacy policy applies to what you give it. Business card to contact opens your phone’s own new-contact screen with the details read from the card; DocFlux does not read or write your contacts.

Data that stays on your device

Some information is stored only on your phone so the app can work and remember your settings. It is not sent to Mobile Castle:

  • Your preferences: for example theme, language, scan and save options.
  • Recent files: off until you turn it on in Settings. When on, the names of the PDFs you opened and Android’s permission to reopen them, so you can open them again from the list.
  • Save places: the last places you saved to, so the save picker can open there.
  • Recently used tools: tool names only, no files.
  • Rewarded uses: how many uses you have earned by watching an ad and not used yet.
  • Language packs you downloaded for translation (about 30 MB each).
  • A local diagnostic log: a technical log of app events and errors in the app’s own folder, to help find problems. It never contains file names, document text, recognised text, passwords or page counts, and it is not uploaded.

Settings > Clear all local data deletes the settings, the recent files list, the working copies and the downloaded language packs (files you saved stay). Uninstalling DocFlux removes everything it stored.

Google ML Kit (scanner, text recognition, translation)

DocFlux uses Google’s ML Kit on your phone:

  • the document scanner (it runs inside Google Play services; DocFlux never gets access to your camera and receives only the finished pages);
  • text recognition (OCR), with a model built into the app;
  • translation and language identification, with language packs downloaded from Google.

ML Kit processes your pages and text on the device. According to Google, ML Kit collects, for diagnostics and usage analytics: device information (such as manufacturer, model, OS version), app information (package name, version), per-installation identifiers (and, for the scanner, which runs in Google Play services, a device identifier), performance metrics, API configuration, input and output sizes, feature version, event types and error codes; for language identification, the languages identified, and for translation, the source and target languages. Translation also uses Firebase Installations (a per-installation identifier that does not identify you or your device) and Firebase Remote Config. This data is encrypted in transit, and Google says ML Kit does not transfer it to third parties. It never includes your documents, pictures or their text.

  • ML Kit data disclosure: https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/android-data-disclosure
  • Firebase privacy: https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy

Language packs. Before a language is used for the first time, DocFlux asks you to download its pack (about 30 MB) from Google. The download happens only after you agree, and only over Wi-Fi unless you allow mobile data. After that, translation works offline. Translation is powered by Google Translate (https://cloud.google.com/translate); machine translation can be wrong, so check it before you rely on it.

Advertising (Google AdMob)

DocFlux is free and supported by ads served through Google AdMob, a Google service. Banners appear only on the Tools and Files tabs and on the result screen after a tool has finished, never over your pages, in a tool, the scanner, a password field or a text result. There are no full-screen ads when you open the app or move between screens. You may choose to watch a rewarded ad to go over a free limit once or to recognise text once; this is always optional.

The ad requests DocFlux sends carry no content from your documents: no file, file name, page, text or translation is ever passed to AdMob. According to Google, the Google Mobile Ads SDK collects and shares, for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention:

  • your IP address, which may be used to estimate your general location;
  • app interactions related to ads (for example ad views and taps, app launch);
  • diagnostic information (for example launch time, hang rate, energy use);
  • device identifiers: the Android advertising ID and the app set ID.

This data is encrypted in transit. Where required by law (for example in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland), DocFlux uses Google’s User Messaging Platform to ask for your consent before any ad is requested, and you can change your choice at any time in Settings > Privacy options. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone’s settings (Settings > Google > Ads, or Security & privacy > Ads on newer Android versions).

  • Google Privacy Policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy
  • How Google uses information from apps that use its services: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites
  • Google Mobile Ads SDK data disclosure: https://developers.google.com/admob/android/privacy/play-data-disclosure Settings also has a More apps row that opens Mobile Castle’s page on Google Play; it is a link, not an ad.

Purchases

This version of DocFlux offers no in-app purchases. It does not connect to Google Play Billing, it asks for no billing permission, and no part of the app can start a payment. Tools marked PRO show “Premium features are coming soon.” If purchases are added in a later version, this policy will be updated before that version is released.

App permissions

DocFlux requests these Android permissions:

  • Internet (INTERNET) and network state (ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE): for ads, ML Kit’s language packs and usage statistics. Documents are not sent.
  • Added by Google’s libraries: the advertising ID and Android’s privacy-preserving ad APIs (AD_ID, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_AD_ID, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_ATTRIBUTION, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_TOPICS) for ads, and background-work permissions (WAKE_LOCK, RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED, FOREGROUND_SERVICE) that these libraries use for their own short tasks.

DocFlux asks for no camera, storage, contacts, location, microphone, phone or SMS permission.

Children

DocFlux is a general-audience utility and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has used the app in a way that raises a concern, please contact us using the details below.

Data retention and deletion

Data DocFlux stores (preferences, the recent files list, save places, language packs, the diagnostic log) stays on your phone until you remove it, with Settings > Clear all local data or by uninstalling the app. Working copies of your files are deleted automatically, at the latest a day after use.

Mobile Castle holds no copy of your data, so there is no server-side deletion to request from us. For data collected by Google’s services (for example advertising data), use the controls Google provides (see its policies above) and your phone’s Ads settings.

Security

Because DocFlux processes your documents on your own phone and Mobile Castle runs no server for them, your documents are not exposed on our infrastructure. Passwords you set with Password protect use AES-256; DocFlux cannot recover a forgotten password. No method of electronic storage or transmission is 100% secure; while we aim to protect your information, we cannot guarantee absolute security.
