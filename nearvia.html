Nearvia — Privacy Policy

Privacy Policy for Nearvia

Effective date: 29 October 2026

Nearvia is published by Mobile Castle.

Summary (plain language)

Nearvia turns your phone into a temporary local server so that devices in the same room can send and receive files. Your files go from your phone to the device in the room and nowhere else. There is no Nearvia server, no Nearvia account, and no cloud storage. File transfer works with the Internet switched off entirely.

Nearvia is free and shows advertisements. This version offers no in-app purchases. The advertising is the one part of the app where something leaves your phone that is not a file you chose to send, and the section below says exactly what that is.

One thing to read before you use the browser option: a guest connecting from a web browser is not on an encrypted connection. Details under Security.

Information Nearvia itself collects

None. Nearvia has no account, no sign-in, no analytics and no telemetry. It does not send your files, their names, their contents or their sizes anywhere, and it has nowhere to send them to: there is no Nearvia backend.

What leaves your phone, and where

Two things, and only two.

  1. The files you choose to share, which go directly to the device you shared them with, over your own local network or over Bluetooth. They do not pass through any server.
  2. Requests for advertisements, which go to Google. See Advertising below.

A phone with no Internet connection can still share files. It cannot fetch an advertisement, so it does not show one.

Data that stays on your device

Nearvia keeps a small amount of information on the phone itself, which never leaves it:

  • the list of devices you chose to remember, and what each is allowed to do;
  • a history of transfers that have finished;
  • your settings, including which transport you prefer and your chosen theme;
  • whether advertisements are currently switched off for you, and until when.

Uninstalling Nearvia deletes all of it.

Advertising (Google AdMob)

Nearvia uses Google’s Mobile Ads SDK to fetch and show advertisements, and Google’s User Messaging Platform to ask for the consent that European and UK law requires before that happens.

What is sent to Google:

Your device’s IP address Unavoidable for any Internet request. It also indicates roughly where you are, at the level of a town
An advertising identifier A resettable number identifying this phone to advertisers. You can reset it, or switch it off, in Android’s Settings ▸ Privacy ▸ Ads
How you interact with advertisements Whether one was shown, seen or tapped
Diagnostic information about the device Model, operating-system version, language, and whether the advertisement drew correctly

Google uses this to select advertisements, to measure them, and to detect fraud. Google’s own privacy policy governs what it does with it: https://policies.google.com/privacy.

What is never sent, and cannot be: your files, their names, their contents or their sizes; who you shared with; which devices you have remembered; or anything typed into the app.

A guest’s web browser is never shown an advertisement. Somebody who connects to your phone from a browser installed nothing and agreed to nothing, so nothing is fetched from Google on their behalf.

If you are in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, Nearvia asks for your advertising consent before any advertisement is requested, and respects your answer. Nearvia remains fully usable whichever way you answer.

Purchases

This version of Nearvia offers no in-app purchases. There is nothing to buy, no prices are shown, and no payment can be made. Where a purchase would later appear, the app says that premium features are coming soon.

If purchases are added in a later version, this policy will be updated before that version is published.

Nearvia also offers an optional rewarded advertisement: if you choose to watch one, advertisements are switched off for twenty-four hours. It is never required, and it unlocks no features — every capability in Nearvia is free, including secure authentication, large files, resumed transfers and integrity checking.

Nearvia does not request access to your contacts, your location, your microphone, your call history or your messages.

Third-party services

  • Google Mobile Ads (AdMob) — advertising. https://policies.google.com/privacy
  • Google User Messaging Platform — the consent form shown in the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom.

No other third-party service receives anything from Nearvia.

Children

Nearvia is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect information from them. It has no account system and asks for no personal details.

Data retention and deletion

Everything Nearvia stores is on your own device. You can clear the remembered devices and the transfer history from inside the app, and uninstalling Nearvia removes all of it. Mobile Castle holds no copy of any of it, because none of it is ever sent anywhere.

What Google retains from advertising requests is governed by Google’s privacy policy, linked above.

Security

Nearvia can be reached in two ways, and they are not equally protected.

  • From the Nearvia app — the connection is encrypted, and the app checks that it is talking to the exact phone whose code you scanned.
  • From a web browser — the connection is not encrypted. This exists so that somebody who cannot install an app is not shut out. Nearvia says so on the screen every time, and the option is off unless you turn it on.

Anyone else on the same network could, in principle, observe a browser connection. On a home network that is usually nobody; on public Wi-Fi it may not be. Use the app, rather than a browser, for anything you would not want a stranger on the same network to see.

Access to your phone is granted by a code you show and can stop at any time. Every permission a guest has is enforced on your own phone, not in their browser.
