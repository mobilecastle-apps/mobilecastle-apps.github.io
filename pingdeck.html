PingDeck — Privacy Policy

Privacy Policy for PingDeck

App: PingDeck: Network Tools Package name: com.mobilecastle.pingdeck Publisher: Mobile Castle Effective date: 28 October 2026

This Privacy Policy explains what information the PingDeck app (“PingDeck”, “the app”, “we”, “us”) does and does not handle, and how. Please read it together with the policies of the third parties named below.

By using PingDeck you agree to the practices described here.

Summary (plain language)

  • PingDeck has no user accounts and no sign-up. We do not ask for your name, email, phone number, or any similar personal detail.
  • Your results stay on your device. Diagnoses, scans, tests, lookups and your history are stored only on your phone or tablet; a report leaves your device only when you share it. Mobile Castle does not operate any server that receives them.
  • Network tools send network traffic, by design. When you run a tool, your device talks to the host you choose, to devices on your own network, or to the public services named below. Mobile Castle receives no copy of it.
  • Location is asked for only for Wi-Fi scans. Android shows nearby Wi-Fi networks and the name of your network only to apps with location access. PingDeck asks for it only when you choose Allow (or Show network name) in a Wi-Fi feature, never reads your position, and never stores or sends your location. It asks for no camera, microphone, contacts, storage, phone or SMS permission.
  • PingDeck shows ads through Google AdMob, a third party. To serve ads, AdMob may collect device information and an advertising identifier. Ads never receive your results, addresses, host names or network names.
  • No purchases in this version. PingDeck offers nothing to buy: it does not connect to Google Play Billing, and it does not ask for the billing permission. Premium features are coming soon.

The rest of this policy gives the detail behind that summary.

Information PingDeck itself collects

PingDeck does not collect personal data on its own servers, because Mobile Castle does not run a server that receives your data. Specifically:

  • No account / identity data. There is no login, so we do not collect a name, email address, password, phone number, or profile.
  • No upload of your results. Addresses, host names, network names, scan results, history and reports are not copied to us.
  • No analytics SDK operated by us. PingDeck does not embed our own analytics, crash-reporting or tracking service. (Google AdMob and the other Google services described below are independent third parties with their own data handling.)

What the tools send, and where

PingDeck contacts other computers only when you use a feature that needs it. Each server it contacts sees your device’s public IP address and the request itself, as any internet server does; their own privacy policies apply.

  • Diagnose my network: connections on port 443 and pings to Cloudflare (1.1.1.1) and Google (8.8.8.8), to measure reachability, delay and loss; one plain web request to Google’s connectivitycheck.gstatic.com, to detect a sign-in page; DNS questions for www.google.com, cloudflare.com and wikipedia.org to your network’s own DNS server, and for www.google.com to Cloudflare, Google and Quad9 (9.9.9.9), to compare DNS speed; pings to your router; and, if you allowed location, a Wi-Fi scan on the phone itself.
  • Speed test (off until you turn it on in Settings): a download of up to 25 MB from speed.cloudflare.com during a diagnosis.
  • Ping, Traceroute, Port scan and HTTP & TLS: the host or address you enter receives the test traffic (for traceroute, also the routers along the way). HTTP & TLS fetches the web address you enter.
  • DNS lookup: your question (the name or address you enter) goes to the DNS server you pick: your network’s own, Cloudflare, Google or Quad9; Compare resolvers asks each of them.
  • WHOIS: the app downloads IANA’s public RDAP directory files from data.iana.org (they do not receive the name you look up), then asks the registry those files name about the domain or address you entered.
  • Show public address (only when you tap it): a request to Cloudflare (https://1.1.1.1/cdn-cgi/trace).
  • Devices and Wake-on-LAN: this traffic stays on your local network: probes to the addresses of your own subnet, listening for the names devices announce (mDNS), reverse DNS lookups of local addresses through your network’s DNS server, and the wake-up packet sent to the broadcast address you enter.

The app’s About screen lists these services. Their privacy policies:

  • Cloudflare: https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/ and, for 1.1.1.1, https://developers.cloudflare.com/1.1.1.1/privacy/public-dns-resolver/
  • Google: https://policies.google.com/privacy and, for 8.8.8.8, https://developers.google.com/speed/public-dns/privacy
  • Quad9: https://quad9.net/privacy/policy/

Use the network tools only on networks and devices you own or have permission to test.

Data that stays on your device

Some information is stored locally on your device so the app can work and remember your settings. This data is not transmitted to Mobile Castle, and it is excluded from Android’s cloud backup. It includes:

  • History: the results of diagnoses, device and Wi-Fi scans, tests and lookups. They can contain what you entered (host names and addresses), addresses on your local network, the names of Wi-Fi networks and access points seen in a scan, and names that devices announced. They are saved in a database in the app’s own private storage. This version shows the newest 10 results; older ones stay on the device and are never deleted on their own.
  • Your preferences: for example theme, language, Expert mode, the speed-test switch, favourite tools and recently used targets.
  • Reports you export: a PDF report is written to the app’s private cache so you can share it; earlier ones are deleted once they are a day old. A text report goes straight to Android’s share sheet.
  • A local diagnostic log: PingDeck writes a technical log (app events and errors) to a file in the app’s own folder on your device, to help troubleshoot problems on real hardware. It never contains network names, access-point identifiers, IP addresses, host names, ports, DNS answers or device names, and it stays on your device unless you deliberately share it.

All of it is removed when you uninstall PingDeck. Settings → Delete history removes your saved results, and Clear all local data removes history and settings. See Data retention and deletion below.

Advertising (Google AdMob)

PingDeck is free and supported by ads served through Google AdMob, a Google service. Ads appear as banners on the dashboard (the Diagnose tab), the tool list, History, the subnet calculator and finished results, never while a diagnosis, scan, test or lookup is running and never while Android’s permission prompt is shown. There are no full-screen ads when you open the app or move between screens.

The ad requests PingDeck sends carry no content from the app: no result, address, host name, network name or report is ever passed to AdMob. To select and show ads, Google AdMob (and the Google Mobile Ads SDK included in the app) may collect and process information such as:

  • a device advertising identifier (Android Advertising ID),
  • device and network information (for example device type, operating-system version, approximate location inferred from your IP address, and similar technical data),
  • ad-interaction and app-usage data related to the ads shown,
  • crash and diagnostic information about the ads software itself.

Google uses this information to serve and measure ads and to help prevent fraud and abuse, in accordance with Google’s own policies. Where required by law (for example, for users in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland), PingDeck uses Google’s User Messaging Platform to show a consent form before serving personalised ads, and no ad is requested until that choice is made; you can change your choice at any time in Settings → Privacy options. You may also choose to watch a short rewarded ad to export one report, or to run one port scan of up to 1,024 ports; this is optional, and the app says what the ad unlocks before it plays.

  • Google Privacy Policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy
  • How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites
  • AdMob / Google Ads policies: https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6128543

You can limit ad personalisation and reset your advertising ID in your device settings (Settings → Google → Ads on most Android phones).

This version of PingDeck offers no in-app purchases. It does not connect to Google Play Billing, it asks for no billing permission, and no part of the app can start a payment. Some features are marked for a future Premium version; they show “Premium features are coming soon.” If purchases are added in a later version, this policy will be updated before that version is released.

App permissions and why they are needed

PingDeck requests the following Android permissions. Each is used only for the feature described:

  • Location (ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION, ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION), asked at run time only when you choose Allow (or Show network name) in a Wi-Fi tool, a diagnosis or the dashboard: Android returns Wi-Fi scan results and the name of your network only to apps with precise location. PingDeck never reads your position, and never stores or sends your location. There is no background location.
  • Wi-Fi (ACCESS_WIFI_STATE, CHANGE_WIFI_STATE): to read the connected Wi-Fi’s details and to request a fresh Wi-Fi scan.
  • Internet (INTERNET): for the network tools and to load ads.
  • Network state (ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE): to read the connection, its addresses, router and DNS servers.
  • Added by the Google libraries: the advertising ID and Android’s privacy-preserving ad APIs (AD_ID, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_AD_ID, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_ATTRIBUTION, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_TOPICS) for ads; and background-work permissions (WAKE_LOCK, RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED, FOREGROUND_SERVICE) that the Google libraries use for their own short tasks.

PingDeck asks for no camera, microphone, contacts, storage, phone, SMS, Bluetooth or background-location permission.

Data retention and deletion

Data stored locally by PingDeck (history, recent targets, preferences, exported reports and the diagnostic log) stays on your device and is retained until you remove it. PingDeck never deletes your history on its own.

You can delete this data at any time by:

  • uninstalling the app, which removes all of its local storage, or
  • using Settings → Delete history (saved results) or Clear all local data (history and settings), or deleting a single result from History.

Mobile Castle does not hold a copy of this data, so there is no separate server-side deletion request to make to us.

For data processed by the third parties above (for example Google’s advertising data), please use the controls those parties provide (see their policies linked above) and your device’s Ads settings.

Security

Because PingDeck keeps your data on your own device and does not run a Mobile Castle server, your results are not exposed on our infrastructure. Reports leave your device only when you share them.

No method of electronic storage or transmission is 100% secure; while we aim to protect your information, we cannot guarantee absolute security.
